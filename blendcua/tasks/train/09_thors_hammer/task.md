# 09_thors_hammer

**Thor's hammer**

Reference: reference.png (a three-quarter perspective photo)
Front plate: reference/front_plate.png (a straight front elevation of the same hammer,
upright, orthographic, on a plain background)
Front blueprint: reference/front_blueprint.png (the plate as a drawing: silhouette fill, one
contour, construction strokes for the bands, rivets, collar, wrap ridges and pommel; the
contour polygon and bbox are in front_blueprint.json beside it)

Only a few basic instructions (everything else is yours to plan and work out):-

Model the hammer in the reference: cracked stone head, gold bands with rivets top
and bottom, dark metal collar, wooden handle with a red leather wrap, and a metal
pommel. To make the ridges on leather wrap, use sculpting.

Build from the front plate first. Put reference/front_plate.png in Blender as the front
view, measure it with scripts (the head's outline, the band positions and widths, the
collar, the handle and wrap extents, the pommel etc), and build the hammer in the front orthographic view so that
its silhouette lands on the plate. Only after that turn to make it match to reference.png, in placement, proportions (like say make the handle a bit longer). The plate gives the shapes; the photo has the final say
on proportions.

The stylised look comes from the textures more than the mesh, so keep the geometry
simple and put the cracks, wear and wood grain into materials and texture painting.

No need for fancy lighting -- keep it simple. Same for the camera: pick a
sensible view that shows the object and move on. Do not spend actions hunting
an exact match to the reference framing.
