18_thors_hammer: three candidate prompts for the upright front plate
====================================================================

Target for all three: the same deliverable as 00_upright_front.png, i.e. the
tilted three-quarter reference turned upright and seen from the front.

Run each against reference.png, size 1024x1536 (PORTRAIT), quality medium, so
they are directly comparable with the existing 00_upright_front.png.

Each prompt below is written to FOLLOW S_HAMMER, exactly like every other job
in generate_views_gpt_image.py. Do not paste the object description in again.

The closing two sentences are identical in all three, on purpose. The only
thing that varies is how much is said about the head. None of the three uses a
negation. See the note at the bottom.


PROMPT A: say nothing about the head
------------------------------------
Tests whether the failure was caused purely by the false clause in the original
("the two gold bands horizontal") and disappears the moment it is deleted. If A
works, B and C are unnecessary and everything after this is noise.

In the supplied image the hammer is tilted diagonally across the frame. Rotate
it anticlockwise until it stands upright, and show it from straight in front:
the handle vertical with the pommel at the bottom and the head at the top. It
is the same hammer, unchanged apart from the turn: same shape, same
proportions, same materials, same stylised look. Centre it on a flat neutral
light-grey background. No text, no extra objects.


PROMPT B: locate the striking faces
-----------------------------------
A is plus one clause. The clause names where the two striking faces end up,
which fixes the head's orientation without ever mentioning the gold bands or an
angle. The bands are attached to the faces, so their orientation follows on its
own. Tests whether one positive structural clause is enough.

In the supplied image the hammer is tilted diagonally across the frame. Rotate
it anticlockwise until it stands upright, and show it from straight in front:
the handle vertical with the pommel at the bottom, and the stone head lying
horizontally across the top with a flat striking face at each end, one on the
left and one on the right, and the handle rising from the middle of the head's
underside. It is the same hammer, unchanged apart from the turn: same shape,
same proportions, same materials, same stylised look. Centre it on a flat
neutral light-grey background. No text, no extra objects.


PROMPT C: describe the current configuration and require it to survive
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Anchors the head-to-handle relationship to something visible in the input image
rather than to a target the model has to take on trust, and asks for a rigid
turn rather than a re-render. Tests whether pointing at the reference beats
describing the destination.

The hammer is a single rigid object: the stone head is fixed to the handle at
right angles and cannot turn on it, so the head and the handle turn together by
the same amount. In the supplied image the handle points up to the right and
the head's long axis crosses it at right angles, running from upper left to
lower right. Keep that right angle exactly as it is and rotate the whole object
anticlockwise until the handle is vertical with the pommel at the bottom, then
look at it from straight in front. Same shape, same proportions, same
materials, same stylised look. Centre it on a flat neutral light-grey
background. No text, no extra objects.


How to score them
-----------------
Two independent checks. A plate can pass one and fail the other, so record them
separately.

1. Head orientation. Correct: the head is a horizontal block, wider than it is
   tall, a striking face at the extreme left and the extreme right, and the two
   gold bands running VERTICALLY, one near each end. Wrong: the bands run
   horizontally across the top and bottom of the head, which means the head has
   been turned 90 degrees on the handle. That is what 00_upright_front.png did.

   Measured on reference.png for comparison: handle axis 31.8 degrees, gold
   bands 25.9 and 23.5 degrees, head long axis 113.4 degrees. So the bands sit
   within about 7 degrees of the handle, and the head crosses it at 81.6
   degrees. In 00_upright_front.png the handle is at 90 and the bands are at 0,
   i.e. the handle turned 58 degrees anticlockwise while the head turned 23
   degrees clockwise.

   This is worth automating: PCA the red wrap pixels for the handle axis and
   the gold pixels for the band axis, and flag any plate where the angle
   between them moves more than about 10 degrees from the reference. Twenty
   lines, and it catches the whole failure class across every task without
   anyone looking at the images.

2. Is it actually front-on. Upright and front are two separate operations, and
   all three prompts ask for both at once. A plate that comes back upright but
   still at a three-quarter angle has failed check 2 while possibly passing
   check 1. If that happens on all three, split it into two calls: an in-plane
   picture rotation first, then a camera swing off that output.

Also worth noting for check 2: neither 00_upright_front.png nor 01_side.png is
a true elevation. Both look down on the head by roughly 10 degrees, so the top
face is visible in both. Watch for that here too.


On negation
-----------
None of the three says what must not appear, even though the obvious guard
would be "the bands are not horizontal". That is deliberate: image models
handle negation poorly and often reproduce the thing being forbidden.

But that cuts against the evidence in this repo. REVIEW.md Lesson A found that
naming what must NOT be in frame is exactly what fixed 04_datestone_1934/02_top,
06_iron_gate/02_top, 11_umbrella/02_side and 12_tablecloth_drape/02_side. Those
were cases where the model was hiding a surface it did not want to give up,
which may be a different problem from this one.

So if all three fail, the natural fourth cell is B with one sentence appended:

  The two gold bands must NOT run horizontally across the top and bottom of the
  head.

Run that only if it is needed. If it is needed, that is a real finding about
when negation helps and when it backfires, and it belongs in REVIEW.md.
