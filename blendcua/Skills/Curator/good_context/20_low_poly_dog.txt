20_low_poly_dog   (run 20_low_poly_dog_run_001)
==========================================
Human judgement, written after looking at the reference photo, final_render.png, the top and
front views, a few step screenshots and (lightly) the plan. This is what a person says is
wrong, why it went wrong, and what to do about it. Severity: M = major (noticed at once),
m = minor. Classes: plan / exec / review / views (a number the single photo could not give) /
tool (the operation does not exist yet).

1. DEFECTS

#  sev  defect
1  M    The upper body is weird, blocky and flaky. It does not read as a nice low-poly object,
        where the facets are clean and the form is continuous. The seams where head, neck and
        body meet are the ugly part: they are three separate solids butted together.
2  M    The legs needed more work; they are made with minimum effort. The feet are super basic
        cuboids that are clearly a separate model sitting under the leg instead of being a
        continuation of it, and that is what makes them look bad.
3  M    The mouth and tongue. Same issue as the shark mouth: the mouth should have been a cavity
        cut into the head part of the mesh, with the tongue inside it. Instead the tongue is a
        piece stuck on one side of the head, the side facing us, so it only works from the
        camera's angle.
4  m    The ears sink into the head and are the same colour as the head, so you can hardly see
        them. They are also very basic; at least a small curve would have helped.


2. BLAME

#  class          why
1  plan + exec    The thing about low-poly objects is that the majority of the piece is built as
                  one mesh structure, and the supporting things are added on top of it. Here the
                  plan lofts the torso, neck and head as three separate solids: see
                  step_001/screenshots/action_05_dog_core_outline_base.png, _outline.png and
                  _silhouette.png. It did a good job following the photo: the head is nice, the
                  body is nice. But by making them separately their joins came out weird. The
                  outline was right; the construction was wrong.
2  exec           Legs and feet were built as separate parts with minimum effort. The feet should
                  be rounded appendages at the bottom of the legs, part of the same mesh, not a
                  cuboid tacked on. More effort should have gone into the limbs.
3  plan + exec    Mouth and tongue built to one side, toward the camera, to look like the photo
                  rather than to be a mouth. This is the same mistake as the shark mouth
                  (17_shark) and the sneaker laces (14_sneaker): things are built on the visible
                  side instead of where they belong on the object.
4  exec           Ears pushed into the head with no check that they read from the side or from
                  the front.


3. FIX

#  fix
1  Plan step 1: one continuous body mesh from head to rump (head, neck, torso as one loft with
   stations along the spine), not three lofts. Durable: a planner and executor skill that says
   "low poly = one main mesh; add-ons only for real attachments (legs, ears, tail)", and a
   reviewer check that the core is one closed mesh with no butt joints.
2  Loft each leg down into a rounded foot within the same mesh, so the foot is the end of the
   leg. Reviewer check: no limb may be a capped cylinder with a box under it.
3  A cut tool (see 17_shark): cut the mouth into the head mesh from the side view, then place
   the tongue inside the cavity on the centreline so it reads from any angle.
4  Ears as small curved flaps, slightly different shade so they read against the head.


4. OTHER
   The side outline of the body and head is good. The problem is how the parts were assembled,
   not where they are. If the same outline had been built as one mesh with the limbs flowing
   out of it, this would be a decent low-poly dog.
