06_tablecloth_drape   (run 06_tablecloth_drape_run_001; loop rebuild run_002; hand build run_003)
==========================================
Human judgement, written after looking at the reference photo, the renders of run_001 and of
the loop's rebuild (run_002), the checker's list, the blame agent's remedy, the plan's scripts
folder and the executor's (empty) scripts folder. This is what a person says is wrong, why it
went wrong, and what to do about it. Severity: M = major (noticed at once), m = minor.
Classes: plan / exec / review / views / tool.

1. DEFECTS

#  sev  defect
1  M    The pedestal legs are round tubes bent into spiral loops. The photo has broad flat
        cabriole legs: an S-curve with a wide knee at the collar, a narrowing ankle and a
        scroll toe, on a turned column with a bulb and a neck. Everything else in run_001 —
        tabletop, column, cloth at frame 90, framing — is fine.
2  m    The wood is lighter and duller than the photo's lacquer (median 93/54/39 against
        67/36/27, few sharp highlights).

   The checker's two majors (foot profile, drape profile) were the right two things to look
   at; a person would have called the cloth acceptable and put the whole round on the legs.


2. BLAME

#  class          why
1  plan + exec    Nobody read the legs out of the photo. The planner's measure_silhouette.py
                  made a clean mask of the pedestal and reduced it to a bounding box (470x336,
                  0.50 of the cloth width, feet at v 0.916). The plan said "three radial
                  cabriole legs" (run_001) and then "a broad, flattened, carved-looking
                  three-branch support over three broad occupied paths" (the rebuild) and gave
                  one span number to hit. The executor's step-1 scripts folder is empty in
                  both runs; the rebuild's action header says "the scale is deliberately
                  convenient rather than copied from pixels". The shape was typed, twice.
2  review         The step-1 reviewer passed a branch count, a shared root and a depth/width
                  ratio. All three were satisfied by three bent flat hooks.
3  curator        The remedy changed the adjectives and kept the method, and chose R2 from
                  step 1 for both majors at once, so the good cloth was thrown away with the
                  bad legs and two hours produced something worse. The right remedy was the
                  read: mask, row table, the outline traced into a polygon, built from that
                  file, checked by the outline back on the photo — legs only, cloth kept.

   Not a views fault: the photo is a near-front elevation of the legs and the mask was one
   row-walk from the shape. (Contrast the skull, whose cross-section no single photo gives
   and which needed the plates.)


3. FIX

#  fix
1  Trace the legs from the photo and extrude them. Done by hand as run_003 on top of run_001:
   wood mask, row table every 10 px (neck at y 683 half-width 25 px, side toes on the floor
   at y 845, L = 271 px, camera pitch 13.5 deg from the near toe), the left leg's outline as a
   661-pixel boundary resampled to 133 points, mapped into the leg's plane, extruded to the
   front leg's measured thickness (0.124 L), three copies at 120 deg, root buried in the
   column, the column's onion collar compressed to a neck. Side legs within 8-11 px of the
   photo's outline on 1024 px. ~200 lines of image reading, 60 of Blender. Tool: outline_trace.
2  Wood: base colour scaled by (photo/render)^2.2 per channel, roughness 0.30 -> 0.13, clear
   coat 0.3 (0.6 mirrored the grey backdrop and turned the wood grey). Median 76/45/41.
3  For the harness: a construction step with an empty scripts folder is "Try again" on its
   own, and the blame agent's first question is "was the reference measured?" before any
   other class (human-judgement rule 9).


4. OTHER
   The cloth of run_001 was kept as it was: a clean square with long corner points and a
   gentle scallop, close enough that the remaining difference is the camera (closer and
   steeper than the photo's). The curator's five cloth gates (bands of 0.006 in normalised
   height) were tighter than a solver can hit and sent the rebuild into crumpled candidates.
